Papers whose main contribution IS a plus-minus variant
Kharrat, López Peña & McHale — "Plus-Minus Player Ratings for Soccer" (p1706, EJOR 2020)
Three variants of the same regression, differing only in the dependent variable. Shared machinery: split each match into segments where the 22 on-pitch players are constant (new segment at each substitution/sending-off/match boundary); design matrix x_tj = +1 home / −1 away / 0 absent; DV normalized to goals per 90 min; ridge (min ‖αx−y‖² + λ‖x‖²) because in soccer XᵀX is near-singular — only 3 subs per team, ~6 segments/match, the same centre-back pairings all season. They explicitly justify ridge over lasso: ridge shrinks correlated predictors toward each other (identical coefficients at 1/k), lasso picks one — desirable so two always-together players get identical ratings.
- PM — DV = goal difference.
- xGPM — DV = net expected goals per segment. Separate xG model per shot type (penalty / free kick / header / open play), ML classifiers on shot location, view angle, inverse goal distance, time, big-chance flag, opposition GK skill features; Brier score as loss for calibrated probabilities; shooter ability deliberately excluded to avoid feedback loops. Result: penalties ≈ random; GK skills add nothing; game time adds nothing.
- xPPM — DV = change in expected points (3/1/0) across the segment, from an in-play Cox proportional-hazards model of each team's scoring rate depending on goal differential and manpower advantage. Team strengths deliberately not in the hazards model (would double-count players and "punish" players on strong teams).
Tuned (λ, ζ time-weighting) by feeding ratings into an ordered probit match-outcome model,10-fold CV, best Brier 0.292 vs 0.295 for bet365-adjusted market. 129,988 segments, 10,983 players. Findings: estimated home advantage ≈ 0.006 for PM / 0.005 for xGPM (players don't perform differently home/away); red card 1 = −1.25, card 2 = −0.16, card 3 ≈0.01.
Sæbø & Hvattum (pmichakt, NIK 2015) — the first academic APM for soccer
Ridge on a huge segment-based regression, x = (αᵀα + λI)⁻¹αᵀβ, λ=0 = OLS. Soccer-specific tricks: "twelfth man" dummy player for home advantage instead of a HFA coefficient; four dismissal dummy players ("first dismissal", "second dismissal", …) to fill red-card slots with cancellation logic; ratings expressed as marginal contribution to goal difference per 90 minutes; exponential discounting of old observations (α_ij = ±e^{−kt}, β_i = 90(H_i−A_i)e^{−kt}/D_i, k=0 = uniform). Ratings then used as the main performance regressor in transfer-fee models. First red card valued at 1.55.
Matano, Richardson, Pospisil, Eubanks & Qin — "Augmenting Adjusted Plus-Minus with FIFA Ratings" (p1810)
Diagnosis of why soccer APM fails: low substitutions and low scoring → collinearity (two centre-backs indistinguishable) + sparse response. Standard APM recast Bayesian: y|β ~ N(Xβ, σ²), β ~ N(0, τ²) (τ=0.1, σ=1). Augmented APM moves the prior off zero: β|α ~ N(α × rating, τ²), α ~ N(μα, σα²), where rating is the mean-shifted EA SPORTS FIFA overall rating — so the coefficient still means "effect vs the average player", but collinear players are decorrelated by giving more credit to the higher-rated one. Also time-weighted segments y ~ N(tXβ, tσ²) (equivalently estimate y/√t, weight by √t). Out-of-sample MSE of summed segment predictions, 10-fold CV, vs Zero / Intercept / FIFA-only / standard APM: Augmented APM best, standard APM beat FIFA-only in 2 of 3 seasons. R package PlusMinusModels.
Hvattum — "Offensive and Defensive Plus–Minus" (papplsci, Appl. Sci. 2020)
Splits plus-minus into offensive and defensive ratings, defined so Σ offensive ratings of the home team − Σ defensive ratings of the away team ≈ goals the home team actually scored. Solved as an unconstrained quadratic program (following Pantuso & Hvattum). Each segment produces two observations (home-perspective DV = goals by h, away-perspective DV = goals by a), which is what makes the offensive/defensive split identifiable. Adds: red-card dummies (n = 1..4), injury-as-red-card when subs are exhausted, per-competition HFA, per-association competition component, and age as a piecewise-linear function of the player (2 non-zero basis terms per exact age). Observation weight w = w_TIME × w_DURATION × w_GOALS (recency discounting, (d+ρ₂)/ρ₃, and up-weighting segments where the scoreline swings by ≥2 both start and end). Regularization is not plain L2: player ratings shrink toward the weighted average of similar players (defined as those who shared the most minutes with them), the O−D difference shrinks toward the average for the same positions, and age effects shrink toward neighbouring ages — all to fight identifiability. 52k+ matches, 30k+ players, 61,466 variables, 711,453 squared terms, ~6.3 segments/match. Findings: defenders high defensive / forwards high offensive; age affects defense far more than offense (young players relatively worse defensively); a red card hurts goals conceded more than goals scored.
Papers that use plus-minus as a component or comparisonHvattum & Gelade — "Comparing bottom-up and top-down ratings" (pbottomup, IJCSS 2021)
Head-to-head: PM (Pantuso & Hvattum, unconstrained quadratic program) as the top-down system vs VAEP as bottom-up. PM as they describe it: minimize Σ_segments w(s)·(g_obs(s) − Σ_p α_p(s)x_p)² + λ·(regularization over all variables), where regularization shrinks player variables toward the average of players they shared the most minutes with, age variables toward neighbouring ages, and everything else toward zero; segment weight by recency, duration and game state; plus HFA per competition, competition components, age functions. Three experiments:
1. PM beats VAEP on both reliability and validity (VAEP ratings are per-match, hence noisy, and never adjust for opponent/teammate quality).
2. More data does not help much — adding second-tier, European cups and other leagues changed prediction loss from 0.576 → 0.574; removing up to ~8 seasons of data still left PM predicting better than any VAEP. Conclusion: a few seasons suffice.
3. Two hybrids (PM-VAEP, PM-P-INT) do not beat plain PM (paired t-tests, all p ≥ 0.48). Explanation: VAEP aggregated over a segment correlates0.93 with goal difference (because a goal has value 1 by construction), so the hybrids are near-duplicates of PM.
Klaiber & Rössle (ps40537, J. Big Data 2026)
Implements PM as the top-down half of a combined rating. Segments on substitution/red card; presence matrix α = +1/−1/0; DV = home-perspective goal difference per segment, normalized to a 90-minute match; home advantage as a dummy home player; solved by ridge regression (α = 3000) — coefficients are the player ratings. Then MaxAbsScaler on both ratings → PCA on the 2-column matrix, first principal component = combined rating (loadings 0.859 top-down / 0.512 bottom-up, i.e. ~74% explained by PM; the two base ratings correlate only 0.311). Labels top players by Transfermarkt market value (90th percentile, ≥1530 min) and feeds the combined rating plus PM into an ML soft-voting ensemble to predict next-season top-player probability. The combined rating outperformed the top-down and bottom-up ratings individually. Top-5 by PM: Ederson 0.0353, Kimmich 0.0318, Neuer 0.0311, Pavard 0.0299, Ederson 0.0299.
Papers that discuss plus-minus only as related work
Pelechrinis & Winston — Skellam / eLPAR (p1807) — the most substantive critique in the set. They summarize basketball/hockey APM (regression credit allocation, L1/L2 regularization, ESPN RPM's box-score prior; regularization needs many observations — ESPN won't publish before mid-season), then note Kharrat's goal and xG versions use ridge, and criticize exactly that: ridge shrinks coefficients and assigns near-identical ratings to players who share the pitch, which is the norm in soccer. Matano's fix (shrink toward the FIFA video-game rating) is credited as analogous to ESPN RPM's box-score prior. They reject APM entirely and substitute each player with (position, FIFA rating) so the regression is player-agnostic, then fit a Skellam regression for the goal differential (Poisson difference; win/draw/loss probabilities via 3 outcomes) with features = rating differences between interacting lines (home attack vs away defense, midfield vs midfield, home defense vs away attack, keeper vs keeper). From that they derive eLPAR (expected league points added above replacement), using replacement levels from the bottom 10th percentile of contract values per position (GK 68.3, DEF 64.4, MID 64.5, ATT 67.5, EPL 2015-16) and formation-average line values. Code: github.com/kpelechrinis/eLPAR-soccer. Follow-up note: the approach is transferable to sports where collinearity would break a plus-minus model, e.g. American football with Madden ratings.
Wolf, Schmitt & Schuller — "A football player rating system" (pjsa, J. Sports Analytics 2021) — no plus-minus method. Only one sentence of related work: "comparing the results a team produces with and without a certain player, one can determine the impact of the player" (Kharrat 2017) and Schultze & Wellbrock's weighted plus-minus. Their own method is Elo extended to team sports, updated after each match from expected vs actual scores.
The review (Hvattum 2019, IJCSS) — the taxonomy everything else plugs into
Defines the split that the other papers sit on: bottom-up (value individual actions) vs top-down (distribute observed team performance); plus-minus is the canonical top-down family. Traces basketball: Rosenbaum (2004, 82games) as the first published APM — segment matches with no substitutions, regress home-vs-away point/possession differential on player dummies (+1/−1), shrink low-minute players, weight later seasons and crunch time higher, then a 3-step denoising (regress APM on per-40 box scores → "statistical plus-minus" → 60/40 convex combination). Then Sagarin & Winston (WINVAL, and WINVAL Impact substituting change in win probability), Ilardi's offensive/defensive split (two observations per segment, one per team's offense), Ilardi & Barzilai's multi-season weighting, Sill's ridge/Tikhonov, Omidiran's penalties toward the median rating, Fearnhead & Taylor's Bayesian separate offense/defense Gaussians with time-varying strengths, Deshpande & Jensen's win-probability-change DV with Laplacian priors + MCMC, and ESPN's Real Plus-Minus ≈ RAPM + statistical PM. Hockey origin: Montréal Canadiens 1950s, Emile Francis; NHL records from 1959/60. Its soccer section catalogues Bohrmann 2011 (nothing significantly different from zero), Hamilton 2014 (ridge, weak predictive power), Sæbø & Hvattum 2015 (first academic APM for soccer), Kharrat's three DVs, Schultze & Wellbrock 2018 (non-regression weighted plus/minus), Sittl & Warnke 2016 (hierarchical fixed effects), Vilain & Kolkovsky 2016 (ordered probit, role-constrained), and Matano 2018 (FIFA-centered prior).
No plus-minus at all
VAEP (p1802_07127), xT-vs-VAEP comparison (pxtvaep), and PlayeRank (p1802_04987) — all pure bottom-up action-value models with no team-level distribution step.
The through-line across the set: every PM variant is the same skeleton (segment the match → regress a team-level DV on ±1 player dummies → regularize for collinearity), and the four axes of innovation are the DV (goals / xG / expected points / Skellam goal diff), the regularizer (ridge → Bayesian FIFA prior → similarity-based shrinkage), the covariates (HFA as dummy player, red-card dummies, competition, age), and the offense/defense split.
